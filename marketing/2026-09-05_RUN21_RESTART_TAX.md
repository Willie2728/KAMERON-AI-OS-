## Campaign

**The Restart Tax**

Hook: **The expensive part of an interrupted AI workflow may be the work you have to redo.**

KAMERON's source-of-truth architecture is built around checkpoint-aware, recoverable autonomous execution: supervise long-running jobs, serialize trustworthy state, protect checkpoint integrity, validate recovery eligibility, and resume from the last approved state instead of automatically starting from zero.

That creates a useful enterprise buying question before a resilience pilot:

- Which workload is expensive to restart from zero?
- What state would have to be reconstructed after interruption?
- What dependencies or permissions must be revalidated before resume?
- What would count as a trustworthy recovery in the pilot?

### Production-ready LinkedIn copy

Most teams measure whether an AI workflow finished. Fewer measure what interruption costs them when the workflow has to start again.

For a long-running agentic task, the hidden tax can be duplicated model calls, repeated tool work, reconstructed context, repeated human review, and time lost rebuilding a trustworthy state.

KAMERON AI OS is being built around a narrower resilience question: can an approved workload preserve trustworthy execution state and resume only after recovery eligibility is revalidated?

Start with the workload you cannot afford to redo from zero. Define the recovery criteria before the pilot. Then test whether the checkpoint actually deserves to resume.

CTA: **Choose one high-value workload for a KAMERON recovery pilot.**

## Claims boundary

Do not claim zero downtime, guaranteed recovery, quantified savings, universal compatibility, or automatic safe resume. External workload recovery requires the KAMERON runtime/SDK/adapter where the task executes.
